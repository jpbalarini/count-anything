Photorealistic industrial produce packing line, viewed from a fixed overhead machine-vision camera mounted directly above the conveyor.

Yellow rectangular plastic produce crates move steadily from left to right along a metal roller conveyor. Each crate contains approximately 10–16 fresh apples packed loosely together, similar to a real fruit processing and packing facility.

The apples have natural variation in appearance: mostly red apples with some green and yellow apples, slightly different sizes, orientations, shapes, and surface textures. Some apples partially overlap or touch each other, but each individual apple should remain visually identifiable.

The camera is positioned almost perfectly top-down at a 90-degree angle, similar to an industrial inspection camera. The framing should closely focus on a single section of the conveyor, with approximately one full crate visible at a time and parts of the previous or next crate occasionally entering the frame.

The conveyor moves continuously at a constant slow speed. The entire crate and all apples inside it move together naturally. Apples must remain stationary relative to their crate while the crate travels through the scene. No apples should randomly move, roll, disappear, appear, morph, or change position inside the crate.

The yellow crates should maintain exactly the same rectangular shape and dimensions throughout the video. New crates periodically enter the frame from one side and exit from the opposite side, creating a continuous production line.

Surrounding environment: realistic stainless-steel machinery, industrial roller conveyors, metal flooring, machine components and structural supports visible around the edges of the frame. Clean but clearly industrial food-processing environment.

Bright neutral factory lighting, high visual clarity, sharp focus, minimal motion blur, realistic textures and shadows.

The video should look like genuine footage captured by an overhead computer-vision camera in an operating produce factory, not a cinematic commercial.

Designed specifically for demonstrating computer vision applications including:
individual apple detection, instance segmentation, apple counting, crate detection, crate tracking, and associating individual apples with each moving crate.
